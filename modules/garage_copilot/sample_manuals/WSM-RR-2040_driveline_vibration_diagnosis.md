# Driveline Vibration Diagnosis — Speed-Dependent Vibration

## Scope

Applies to speed-dependent vibration complaints in the 40–120 km/h band on
Ghost, Phantom and Cullinan platforms. Entry condition: a completed road test
per WSM-RR-1005 with the complaint characterised by speed band, felt location,
and load behaviour.

## Decision sequence

Work the causes in the order below. Do not skip to component replacement
without completing the discriminating check for each stage.

### Stage 1 — Wheel and tyre imbalance

Typical signature: vibration proportional to road speed, felt in the steering
wheel between 100 and 120 km/h for front imbalance, or in the seat/floor for
rear imbalance. Amplitude is broadly the same under drive, cruise and coast.

Check: dynamic balance all four wheels per WSM-RR-2010 and inspect each rim
for runout. If the complaint band is 55–70 km/h and amplitude changes with
load, imbalance is NOT the primary suspect — proceed to Stage 2 before
fitting balance weights.

### Stage 2 — Constant-velocity (CV) axle joints

Typical signature: vibration or shudder strongest between 50 and 75 km/h,
increasing under drive load and easing on coast; may be accompanied by a
click on full-lock manoeuvres (outer joint) or a shudder on take-off (inner
joint, plunge type). A worn inner CV joint on the front axle commonly presents
as a body/floor vibration around 60 km/h under light load and is frequently
misdiagnosed as wheel imbalance.

Check: inspect both front CV axles per WSM-RR-2041 — boot condition, grease
leakage, joint play at the inner tripod housing. Refer to TSB-RR-2026-03 for
the known Ghost II production batch affected by premature inner-joint wear.

### Stage 3 — Propshaft and centre bearing

Typical signature: drone or vibration through the floor from 80 km/h upward,
insensitive to steering input, sometimes with a launch clunk. Check propshaft
balance weights present, centre-bearing rubber for cracking, and universal
joint play.

### Stage 4 — Brake disc thickness variation

Typical signature: vibration only while braking from speed. Not a driveline
cause; route to the brake judder procedure.

## Common misdiagnosis note

A complaint of "vibration at about 60 km/h" has three realistic causes in
order of observed frequency on Ghost platforms: front inner CV joint wear,
wheel/tyre imbalance with a bent rim, and propshaft centre bearing. Balancing
the wheels resolves the complaint in fewer than one case in three when the
onset band is 55–70 km/h and the amplitude is load-sensitive. Complete the
Stage 2 CV-axle inspection before releasing the vehicle after a balance-only
repair.
