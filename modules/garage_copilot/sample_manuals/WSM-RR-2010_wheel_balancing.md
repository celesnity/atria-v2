# Wheel and Tyre Dynamic Balancing — Procedure and Limits

## When balancing applies

Dynamic imbalance produces a vibration whose amplitude rises with road speed
and is typically most pronounced between 100 and 120 km/h. Front-wheel
imbalance is felt in the steering wheel rim; rear-wheel imbalance through the
seat and floor. Imbalance vibration is load-insensitive: it persists
essentially unchanged whether the vehicle is driven, cruised, or coasted
through the speed band.

## When balancing does not apply

Do not expect balancing to resolve:

- Load-sensitive vibration (stronger under drive, easing on coast) in the
  50–75 km/h band — characteristic of CV axle inner-joint wear; see
  WSM-RR-2040 Stage 2 and WSM-RR-2041.
- Vibration only under braking — brake disc thickness variation.
- Drone above 80 km/h insensitive to steering input — propshaft or centre
  bearing.

Fitting balance weights against these signatures loses workshop time and
erodes customer confidence when the symptom returns on the delivery drive.

## Procedure

1. Mount the wheel on the balancer with the correct centring cone and pressure
   plate. Wheel must be clean; remove stones from the tread.
2. Measure rim lateral and radial runout first. Service limit: 0.8 mm lateral,
   0.6 mm radial at the bead seat. A rim beyond limits cannot be balanced into
   an acceptable state; replace or refurbish the rim.
3. Balance to a residual of 5 g or better per plane. Use adhesive weights on
   the inner barrel positions specified for each rim design; clip-on weights
   are not approved for forged rims.
4. Refit per the wheel fitment procedure with new single-use wheel bolts,
   torque 140 Nm star pattern.
5. Confirm by road test through the customer's reported speed band per
   WSM-RR-1005.
