# Road Test Procedure for NVH Complaints

## Purpose

This procedure standardises the road test used to characterise a customer
vibration, noise, or harshness (NVH) complaint before any component is
replaced. A correctly characterised complaint eliminates at least half of the
candidate causes before the vehicle returns to the workshop.

## Preconditions

- Repair Order (RO) opened and the customer complaint recorded verbatim.
- Tyre pressures set to the placard values while cold.
- Fuel level between one quarter and three quarters; no loose load in the
  luggage compartment.
- Road test route must include a smooth section allowing a steady 40–120 km/h.

## Step 1 — Reproduce the complaint

Drive the route and attempt to reproduce the symptom exactly as the customer
described it. Record:

- The speed band in which the symptom appears and disappears (e.g. appears at
  55 km/h, strongest at 60–65 km/h, fades above 75 km/h).
- Whether onset correlates with road speed or engine speed. Hold vehicle speed
  constant and change gear: if the vibration character does not change with
  engine RPM, the source rotates with road speed (wheels, tyres, brake discs,
  driveshafts, CV axles).
- Where the vibration is felt: steering wheel rim, seat cushion or floor pan.
  Steering-wheel vibration points to the front axle corner components;
  seat/floor vibration points to the rear axle or the propshaft/driveline.

## Step 2 — Load and coast discrimination

Within the symptomatic speed band, perform three passes:

- Steady throttle (cruise) — note vibration amplitude.
- Light acceleration under load — note whether amplitude increases.
- Coast in neutral through the band — note whether amplitude collapses.

A vibration that is present at steady cruise and unchanged when coasting is
characteristic of wheel/tyre imbalance or a bent rim. A vibration that
increases markedly under drive load and reduces when coasting is
characteristic of a driveline joint — inspect the constant-velocity (CV)
axles and propshaft joints before balancing wheels (see WSM-RR-2040).

## Step 3 — Steering input discrimination

In the symptomatic band, apply a gentle lane-change weave. If the vibration
amplitude changes with cornering load transfer, suspect a wheel-end component
(bearing, CV joint). If unchanged, suspect rotating-mass imbalance.

## Recording

Record all observations on the RO before returning the vehicle to the bay.
The road-test record is mandatory input to the driveline vibration diagnosis
procedure WSM-RR-2040.
